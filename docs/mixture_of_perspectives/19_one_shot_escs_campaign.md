# One-Shot ESCS Campaign

**Status:** frozen historical execution contract; current live authority is the Generation-1 campaign record
**Campaign schema:** `mop-escs-one-shot-campaign/v1`
**Snapshot date:** 2026-07-12
**Scope:** Event-Sourced Coalition Substrate (ESCS), prerequisite experiments, integration, and promotion
**Optimization target:** maximize verified intelligence produced per unit of computation, not model size or aggregate compute

> **Outcome supersession, 2026-07-13:** This document preserves the preregistered one-shot design and must not be rewritten as though later outcomes were known in advance. Current execution state, the Generation-1 discovery census, and the accepted post-run synthesis child are governed by [the Generation-1 empirical campaign](./20_generation1_empirical_campaign.md) and their sealed proof artifacts. Later evidence established X0 as a strong null for its tested learner, EDCM-1/X1 as an invalid complementarity bed, and P6-10k as a joint retention/future-learning null that blocks its larger rungs. None establishes an active or superior integrated ESCS.

This document is the execution contract for taking the existing ESCS mechanics, active prerequisite work, and surviving Mixture-of-Perspectives evidence through an autonomous experimental campaign. It is deliberately stricter than an implementation roadmap: every long-running stage has an entry gate, a terminal verdict, and an explicit route after positive, null, failed, blocked, or invalid-bed outcomes.

The campaign may integrate a mechanism under the permissive candidate/control gate without claiming that the mechanism is scientifically established. Scientific promotion is a separate, substantially harder gate. Passing the first never implies passing the second.

Live proof artifacts and current verifier outputs supersede this snapshot when they disagree, but a campaign controller must not silently reinterpret a stage. Any changed prerequisite, policy, metric, or threshold requires a new sealed campaign revision.

## 1. Evidence vocabulary

| Code | Meaning | Campaign treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `M` | Verified mechanics or infrastructure only | May be used as audited campaign machinery; supports no architectural efficacy claim. |
| `T+` | Fresh-seed verified toy or programmatic positive | May enter as a feature-flagged candidate, never as a promoted natural-world result. |
| `N` | Controlled null, tie, or control-favored result | Retain as a baseline, negative constraint, or retired mechanism; do not relabel it as support. |
| `F` | Failed verifier or invalid experimental bed | Exclude from inference and downstream scientific gates until repaired and resealed. |
| `P` | Pending, active, or not yet independently verified | Cannot satisfy a downstream positive dependency. |
| `B` | Blocked by rights-clean data, upstream evidence, or implementation | May exist only as a non-claiming stub until the block is removed. |

These codes describe evidence, not software completeness. A component can be fully implemented while remaining `M`, `N`, or `P` scientifically.

## 3. Architectural hypothesis under test

ESCS treats intelligence as a sparse, event-driven organization of heterogeneous perspectives. It does not assume uniform token compute, simultaneous module activation, a single reasoning route, continuous processing, or a linguistic universal representation.

```text
substrate and perspective adapters
    -> charged RawPacket
    -> EventFormerPolicy
    -> ObservationEvent or HypothesisEvent
    -> EventLedger and causal-factor frontier
    -> bounded candidate retrieval
    -> DispatchPolicy
    -> temporary Actor coalition
    -> typed ClaimMessages and endogenous HypothesisEvents
    -> ActionIntent, memory operation, deletion, or topology proposal
    -> CommitmentEvent
    -> ExternalEffect
    -> ConsequenceEvent
    -> actor-local credit and stage_update
    -> archive, compact, or erase
    -> rare transactional topology manager
```

The shared substrate is not a universal latent. It is a narrow organizational contract comprising:

- event, referent, branch, provenance, and schema identity;
- commitment and consequence authority;
- typed claims and bounded communication;
- lifecycle charging for success, error, idle time, retention, archive, deletion, and structural mutation;
- explicit counterfactual versus actual branches;
- reversible, governed structural change.

No actor may silently alter factual state. Claims become externally consequential only through a charged commitment followed by a recorded consequence.

## 4. Perspective interfaces

| Cognitive function | ESCS role | Required interface or constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Visual, audio, language, symbolic, programmatic, telemetry | `SubstrateAdapter` or `PerspectiveAdapter` | Emit audited `RawPacket` records with source, time, schema, provenance, and charge; never expose evaluator labels. |
| Event segmentation and temporal change detection | `EventFormerPolicy` | Convert meaningful change into observation or endogenous hypothesis events; compare against periodic and always-on controls. |
| Reactive, spatial, motor, and intuitive response | Cheap `Actor` baseline | Remain eligible at low cost and provide the no-deliberation control. |
| Probabilistic reasoning | `Actor` | Emit calibrated typed claims with uncertainty and evidence references. |
| Episodic and semantic memory | `Actor` plus `BoundedArchive` query | Preserve provenance, freshness, deletion, revision, and retrieval charges. |
| Causal reasoning | Branch-aware `Actor` | Make intervention-specific claims and keep counterfactual branches separate from actual state. |
| Planning, simulation, and imagination | Endogenous hypothesis events | Simulations remain counterfactual until an `ActionIntent`; P7 action benefit is mandatory. |
| Verification, contradiction detection, reflection, self-critique | Verifier `Actor` | Use bounded rounds; cannot silently rewrite another actor's claim or factual state. |
| Confidence and uncertainty estimation | Readiness metadata or monitor actor | May inform arbitration but cannot be the sole compute trigger. |
| Curiosity and novelty | Coverage debt, reducible-value estimate, or task-relevant feature | Must beat noisy-TV and irreducible-surprise controls; raw novelty is prohibited as an activation rule. |
| Arbitration and coalition formation | `DispatchPolicy` | Select bounded actors under explicit value-of-computation, caps, and exact-credit controls. |
| Learning and plasticity | `Actor.stage_update` after `ConsequenceEvent` | Only causally involved actors update; inactive actors do not receive hidden gradient or reward. |
| Structural adaptation | Finite `G0` topology manager | Shadow, canary, commitment, consequence, rollback, and mutation budget are mandatory. |
| External action | `ExternalEffect` | Only committed actions may affect the environment. |
| Self-monitoring | P9-derived monitoring actor | Reports causal and resource anomalies without privileged control authority. |

## 5. The two non-interchangeable gates

### 5.1 Gate A: permissive candidate/control integration

A mechanism may enter the scaffold only when all applicable items below hold:

1. Event, source, config, code, schema, branch, referent, and mutation identities are exact and deterministic.
2. Actor, message, round, memory, archive, retention, structural, and wall-time caps are explicit.
3. Success, error, abstention, idle work, archive, deletion, retry, and retention are charged rather than omitted.
4. Evaluator labels, future information, hidden state, and control-only information cannot leak into candidates.
5. The preregistration contains a non-vacuous null and a strongest-known matched control.
6. The interface is replaceable and does not encode promotion language or assume a positive result.
7. Evidence status remains attached to the component and propagates to downstream artifacts.

Gate A routing is intentionally permissive:

- `M`: integrate as audited infrastructure only.
- `T+`: integrate behind a feature flag as a bounded candidate.
- `N`: integrate only as a baseline, control, or explicitly retired mechanism.
- `F`: exclude until repaired and resealed.
- `B`: expose only a non-claiming stub that cannot satisfy a dependency.
- `P`: scaffold if useful, but do not activate it as a positive premise.

### 5.2 Gate B: strict scientific promotion

Promotion requires all applicable conditions below; satisfying Gate A is only the first prerequisite.

1. The task is non-ceiling and contains independently verified complementarity or another non-vacuous architectural demand.
2. Data are rights-clean and natural or independently generated where the claim requires that scope.
3. Train, selection, test, and fresh verification are disjoint by session, world, referent, and contamination boundary.
4. Controls include the strongest matched, tuned, random, shuffled, oracle, fixed-policy, and cheaper-system alternatives relevant to the mechanism.
5. At least five registered seeds plus a disjoint fresh replication survive; confidence intervals clear the SESOI and the conclusion does not flip under the registered sensitivity analyses.
6. An independent verifier reproduces the result from sealed artifacts and checkpoints, preferably through a separate implementation path.
7. Lifecycle work, bytes, wall time, peak memory, and energy are measured whenever an efficiency claim uses them.
8. Noisy TV, poison, revision, deletion, schema change, event storm, lesion, restoration, abstention, and relevant adversarial cases pass.
9. Prediction and simulation produce registered intervention or action benefit rather than forecast quality alone.
10. The current governor authorizes the exact configuration and all wording is bounded to the tested population, substrate, horizon, and intervention.

Failure at Gate B does not erase a valid Gate A result. It fixes the wording and prevents architectural promotion.

## 9. Autonomous long-run schedule

Only one unmeasured resource probe or claim-bearing heavy comparison may run at a time. This is an evidence
constraint as well as a resource constraint: it prevents co-resident work from changing wall time, memory
pressure, governor state, or reproducibility. After both lanes have independent resource receipts, the
controller may overlap one MPS producer with one CPU/light/network task only when the governor admits the
pair, their dependency chains and output globs are disjoint, neither result uses co-resident wall time or
energy as a scientific endpoint, and the campaign receipt records the overlap. Otherwise heavy work remains
serial. Supported tasks should use their measured fastest CPU or MPS backend internally rather than leave an
accelerator idle by convention.

The default heavy sequence is:

1. Preserve the sealed terminal-null P5 verifier as a control/constraint; do not relabel it positive.
2. Freeze governor and policy compatibility.
3. Run and independently verify official EDCM-1.
4. Run X0.
5. Run X1 only after EDCM complementarity is adjudicated.
6. Run the exclusive P6 ladder: 10k, then 100k, then 1m, advancing only after each verifier-positive terminal verdict.
7. Run X2 after its minimum P6-10k, P5, P7, and control prerequisites pass.
8. Freeze finite G0, then run X3 only if its entry conditions survive.
9. Run the integrated ESCS campaign only after X0 and at least two of X1, X2, and X3 survive separately.
10. Run natural/session-disjoint confirmation, then strict promotion review.

Every heavy node must have:

- a governor task and current prerequisite seals;
- an admission record showing acceptable CPU load, memory pressure, thermal state, disk space, and absence of another heavy campaign;
- a checkpoint/resume boundary that does not alter experimental ordering;
- an independent verifier task separated from the producer;
- a proof manifest containing config, code identity, seeds, inputs, outputs, resource telemetry, and terminal verdict.

If resources are temporarily unavailable, the controller waits and retries admission. It does not reduce seeds, horizons, controls, precision, or metrics to make a run fit. If a process is interrupted after a valid checkpoint, it resumes from that checkpoint under the same seal; otherwise it restarts the stage. A terminal null or killed premise is never auto-retried with a modified hypothesis.

## 11. Measurement and evaluation contract

Each proposed mechanism is evaluated along all ten requested dimensions. A stage may mark an item `not_applicable`, but it may not omit it.

| Dimension | Minimum recorded evidence |
|---|---|
| Biological plausibility | Which biological principle is mimicked, which is deliberately abstracted away, and whether the result depends on the analogy. |
| Computational plausibility | Explicit state, update, communication, and asymptotic cost model. |
| Engineering feasibility | Failure recovery, observability, deterministic replay, replaceability, and operational complexity. |
| Scaling behavior | Registered horizon, actor, event, memory, and communication scaling curves. |
| Sample efficiency | Utility versus examples, events, interventions, and environment interactions. |
| Reasoning quality | Task utility, calibration, intervention ranking, action value, and scope-specific accuracy. |
| Robustness | Distribution shift, noisy TV, poison, deletion, revision, schema change, event storm, lesion, restoration, and abstention. |
| Interpretability | Provenance-complete event-to-claim-to-commitment-to-consequence trace and coalition rationale. |
| Emergent behavior | Predeclared beneficial and hazardous phenomena, detected without post-hoc relabeling. |
| Computational efficiency | Fully charged lifecycle work, bytes, wall time, peak memory, energy where available, and Pareto comparison to strongest controls. |

Primary integrated reporting is a Pareto surface, not a single scalar. At minimum it includes task/action utility, lifecycle work, communicated bytes, retained state, wall time, peak memory, and calibration. An efficiency result is positive only when the registered utility tolerance and resource improvement are both satisfied.
