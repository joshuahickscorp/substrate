# Q2 — Value-of-Computation / Adaptive-Compute: Honest Saving-at-Parity

**(a) Accuracy-vs-total-compute Pareto + rate-matched random baseline.** Plot quality (accuracy / −loss) on **y** vs **total compute** on **x**, reporting compute as a *set* of non-co-moving indicators (params + FLOPs + throughput/wall-clock) because they disagree and rankings reorder across them (*The Efficiency Misnomer*, Dehghani et al., **ICLR 2022**). Sweep the gate knob (halting threshold, exit-confidence, ponder-cost λ, skip ratio) to trace (compute, accuracy) points; the **Pareto frontier** = non-dominated set. Scalars: area-under-curve, **accuracy@budget** (iso-FLOP slice), **compute@accuracy** (iso-accuracy slice).
*Rate-matched random baseline (exact):* a gate that fires the **same number of times / same activation rate ρ, same expected FLOPs B**, but places firing positions **uniformly at random, matched per-example/per-seed** so the compute histogram is identical; any accuracy gap is then attributable to **where** compute is spent, not how much. This is exactly the control in **SkipNet** (Wang et al., **ECCV 2018**: "for the same model density, learned gating … performs significantly better than random gating") and **BlockDrop** (Wu et al., **CVPR 2018**: *RandomK* keeps K blocks at random — CIFAR-10/ResNet-110 RandomK ≈14.5% vs learned 75.4% at matched ~20 blocks). Honest saving-at-parity = candidate frontier **strictly dominates** the rate-matched-random frontier.

**(b) Named designs beating a rate-matched random gate (verified):**
- **SkipNet** (ECCV 2018, arXiv:1711.09485): **30–90% compute reduction** at preserved accuracy; **at matched density beats random gating**.
- **BlockDrop** (CVPR 2018, arXiv:1711.08393): ImageNet ResNet-101 holds **76.4% top-1 at 1.25×10¹⁰ vs 1.56×10¹⁰ FLOPs (≈19.9% fewer, up to 36% per-image)**; dominates RandomK.
- **CALM** (Schuster et al., **NeurIPS 2022**, arXiv:2207.07061): confidence-triggered early exit, **up to ×3 speedup "while provably maintaining high performance"** (T5-1.1 on CNN/DM, WMT, SQuAD).
Supporting halting/early-exit: **ACT** (Graves 2016, arXiv:1603.08983) parity error **≈40%→<5%** with small ponder penalty; **PonderNet** (Banino 2021, arXiv:2107.05407) bAbI **1,658 vs 10,161 steps (≈6.1× fewer than UT)** at lower error (0.15±0.9 vs 0.29±1.4), extrapolates to ~99% while ACT stays at chance; **PABEE** (NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2006.04152) **1.57× speedup while improving accuracy** (ALBERT/GLUE 84.4→85.1 dev).
**Correction (DeeBERT, ACL 2020, arXiv:2004.12993):** the "<0.5% drop" holds only for the single best case (QQP: BERT-base −0.1 F1 at 39% saved); at ~40% saved most GLUE tasks lose **~0.6–1.3%** (BERT-base), **up to ~4%** (RoBERTa MNLI −3.9/−4.1). Blanket "<0.5%" is unsupported.

**(c) Irreducible-noise ("noisy-TV") control.** The **noisy-TV problem** — an agent rewarded by raw prediction error is captured forever by irreducible/aleatoric stochasticity — named in Burda et al. *Large-Scale Study of Curiosity* (2018) and **Burda et al. "Exploration by Random Network Distillation" (RND), ICLR 2019** (arXiv:1810.12894); roots in Schmidhuber's artificial curiosity.
- **ICM forward-dynamics prediction-error curiosity** (Pathak et al., ICML 2017, arXiv:1705.05363) → **chases the noisy TV** (stochastic transitions give permanently high error).
- **RND fix:** bonus = predictor error vs a **fixed, deterministic, randomly-initialized target f\*(o)** of the *current* observation; error **→ 0 as predictor learns the fixed map**, so transition-noise stops paying.
- **Learning-progress / information-gain gates** (Schmidhuber 1991; Oudeyer & Kaplan, IEEE TEC 2007) reward the *derivative* of prediction/compression, **zero on irreducible noise** → don't chase it.
*Concrete control:* inject a pure-aleatoric channel (random static / appended white-noise dim with no reducible structure) and **require the gate's firing rate on it = chance** (indistinguishable from base/rate-matched-random). Preferential firing on injected noise ⇒ triggering on irreducible noise → fail.

**(d) Cautionary cases: saving shrinks/inverts under matched-FLOPs.**
- *FLOPs ≠ throughput* — *The Efficiency Misnomer* (ICLR 2022) Table 1: **ViT-B/8 = 86.5M params, 78.54 GFLOPs, 7.17 ms** vs **ViT-B/64 = 95.3M params, 0.93 GFLOPs, 0.11 ms** → more params yet **≈84.5× fewer FLOPs and ≈65× faster**. **Matched-FLOPs reversal:** deep **D48 (93.42M, 4.43 GFLOPs, 0.64 ms)** vs wide **W3072 (101.52M, 4.44 GFLOPs, 0.35 ms)** — near-identical FLOPs yet **1.83× throughput gap**, so a matched-FLOPs verdict fully reverses at wall-clock. Same paper: sparse/MoE FLOP cuts of "several orders of magnitude" **do not translate to equal speed-ups** on real hardware.
- *Batching erodes per-example ACT/early-exit savings* (wooden-barrel effect): a synchronized batch waits for its deepest non-exited member, collapsing per-example FLOP savings toward ~1× at batch>1. The early-exiting survey (arXiv:2509.05915) notes per-token dynamism "can paradoxically reduce throughput in batched inference"; **HELIOS** (arXiv:2504.10724) reports vanilla EE-LLMs improve throughput only **~16%**; **Apparate** (SOSP '24, arXiv:2312.05385) is built around exactly this tension.
- *Conditional-compute under unoptimized kernel:* **MoEUT** (arXiv:2405.16039, NeurIPS 2024) trains **≈1.5–2× *slower* in wall-clock** than a FlashAttention Transformer (suboptimal Triton kernel) — nominal savings don't (yet) materialize. **Correction:** paper says "1.5–2× slower" (training), not "~1.7×"; a kernel caveat, not a fundamental loss.

**(e) Charging the gate's amortized training cost (full-lifecycle).** Rule from **"Green AI," Schwartz et al., CACM 2020** (arXiv:1907.10597): report **total work/FLOPs incl. developing+training+running**. Amortize controller training cost C_train over the N-query workload → per-query overhead **C_train/N**; honest condition **(per-query FLOPs saved) − C_train/N > 0**, i.e. **cumulative inference FLOPs saved > C_train**, break-even **N\* = C_train / (per-query saving)**. Anchored by **Strubell et al., ACL 2019** (arXiv:1906.02243): NAS-trained big Transformer emits **≈626,155 lbs CO₂e**, establishing training/search cost as first-class. *[The "inference is ~80–90% of lifecycle energy" figure is unverified — no cited source; the amortization argument stands without it.]*

**(d) Minimum positive-event-count per split — rule of thumb.** **≥24 validation / ≥12 test is DEFENSIBLE as a rule of thumb, but no CPD/TSAD benchmark mandates a minimum** *[unverified — none of NAB/TCPD/GEBD state one]*. Grounding: the events-per-variable "one-in-ten rule" (**Peduzzi et al. 1996, J Clin Epidemiol 49(12):1373–1379**; Monte-Carlo over EPV∈{2,5,10,15,20,25}: "no major problem" once EPV>10) → ≥10 positives per estimated quantity; contested by **van Smeden et al. 2016, BMC Med Res Methodol 16:163** ("No rationale for the 1-in-10 rule"). Binomial reasoning: recall/precision are proportions with SE=√(p(1−p)/n_pos); worst-case p=0.5 → n=12 gives 95% CI half-width ≈0.28 (bare floor), n=24 gives ≈0.20. So **≥12 positives = minimum for any stable rate; ≥24 (≈double, for the split where you tune the operating point) holds recall to ≈±0.2 worst-case** — consistent with the ≥10–15-per-condition folklore. Explicitly a rule of thumb.

**(e) Paired-seed tests — summary.** **NONE of these use a paired-seed test.** BOCPD & GEBD: no test (single numbers / qualitative). NAB: single aggregate score. Kim / Wu&Keogh: metric critiques. **TCPD is the only one with a formal test** — Friedman + Wilcoxon signed-rank (Holm) + CD diagrams, **paired across datasets, not seeds** (Demšar 2006 protocol; Friedman df=k−1, Iman–Davenport df=(k−1),(k−1)(N−1)).

# Q4 — Coalition / MoE / Ensemble Advantage at Matched Compute + Honest Credit

**(a) Standard matched-compute design (iso-FLOP + iso-param).** Hold FLOPs (or wall-clock GPU/TPU-hours) constant, vary only the mixture-vs-single axis, plot quality vs a *compute* axis. Two artifacts: an **iso-FLOP curve** (MoE claim = leftward shift) and an **iso-param/iso-cost control** (grow a dense baseline until it matches the mixture's params/FLOPs, separating "more params" from "structure").
- **Switch Transformer** (Fedus et al., *JMLR* 23, 2022, arXiv:2101.03961): top-1 Switch-FFN holds **FLOPs/token = dense T5** while params scale with #experts; dense controls T5-Base ≈223M, T5-Large ≈739M; all on 32 TPUv3, equal FLOPs/example → **up to 7× pre-training speedup at matched compute** (64-expert Switch-Base ≈7B reaches T5-Base quality in 1/7 the time).
- **GShard** (Lepikhin et al., ICLR 2021, arXiv:2006.16668): iso-FLOP top-2 routing; 600B-param MoE at a budget comparable to a much smaller dense model.
- **Scaling Laws for Fine-Grained MoE** (Krajewski/Ludziejewski et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.07871): joint law L(N,D,G) on iso-FLOP compute-optimal fronts; compute-optimal MoE matches a dense model using **~20× more compute at 10²⁰ FLOPs, widening to >40× at 10²⁵**; optimal granularity **budget-dependent, ≈8 (small) → ≈64 (largest)**. *[Corrected from "flat G≈8"; the "G=16 lets routing dominate" sub-claim was unsupported and dropped.]*
- **On Power Laws in Deep Ensembles** (Lobacheva et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2007.08483): memory-split holds total params fixed; calibrated NLL C_NLL(n)=c+b·nᵃ (a<0). Headline: **the equal-budget ensemble usually wins; a single large net is almost never optimal** (evidence *for* ensembles).
- **BatchEnsemble** (Wen et al., ICLR 2020, arXiv:2002.06715): rank-1 per-member factors → K members at ≈1× params of one net — the canonical near-matched-cost ensemble baseline.

**(b) Credit estimators + documented bias.**
*Difference reward / Wonderful-Life / Aristocrat* (Wolpert & Tumer, arXiv:cs/9908014; 2001): **D_i(z) = G(z) − G(z_{−i} + c_i)** with c_i a fixed counterfactual "clamp"; **WLU** = G(z) − G(z_{−i}); **Aristocrat** AU_i = R(a_{−i},a_i) − E_{a′_i∼π_i}[R(a_{−i},a′_i)]. D_i is perfectly factored yet strips other agents' noise → far more learnable than shared G. **Bias:** credit depends on the **choice of clamp c_i**, and G(z_{−i}+c_i) usually must be *estimated*, injecting bias/variance — the problem **COMA** (Foerster et al., AAAI 2018, arXiv:1705.08926) attacks with a centralized-critic counterfactual baseline.
*Shapley:* **φ_i = Σ_{S⊆N\{i}} [|S|!(n−|S|−1)!/n!]·[v(S∪{i})−v(S)]**. **Monte-Carlo/permutation estimator** (exact is O(2ⁿ)): **φ̂_i = (1/M)Σ_m [v(Pre^{σ_m}(i)∪{i}) − v(Pre^{σ_m}(i))]**, **unbiased**, SE **O(1/√M)** (Castro et al. 2009; Mitchell et al., *JMLR* 23, 2022). **Bias/variance:** MC-Shapley variance is high → stratified/antithetic/QMC reduction; regression-based **KernelSHAP** trades unbiasedness for variance — per **Covert & Lee, AISTATS 2021** (arXiv:2012.01536) the original "incurs a small increase in bias for significantly lower variance," and they derive an *unbiased* variant (so the original is **not** established unbiased). *[The verbatim "unclear whether it is unbiased" quote and the "ℓ1 adds further bias" line are unverified.]*

**(c) Minimum evidence bar for real complementarity.**
1. **Per-actor unique-win / niche:** **u_i = (1/N)Σ_n 1[i correct AND all j≠i wrong]**; report Wilson/Clopper–Pearson binomial CI; credit only if **CI lower bound excludes the redundant-expert null** (for k independent experts at accuracy p, expected unique-win = **p(1−p)^{k−1}**; correlated experts → 0).
2. **Utilization/specialization entropy:** H(p)=−Σ_i p_i log p_i over expert usage (+ per-class conditional entropy) — Switch adds a **load-balancing aux loss** precisely because routing collapses.
3. **Diversity–error (ambiguity) identity** (Krogh & Vedelsby, NeurIPS 1995): **(p̄−y)² = (1/B)Σ_b(p_b−y)² − (1/B)Σ_b(p_b−p̄)²** → **ensemble error = avg member error − diversity**; gain equals the diversity term. Classifier analogue: bias–variance–covariance (Ueda & Nakano 1996) error ≈ bias² + (1/M)var + (1−1/M)covar — helps only insofar as members' errors **de-correlate**. Credit the diversity/covariance-reduction term, not raw accuracy.

**(d) Published matched-compute collapse.** **"Deep Ensembles Work, But Are They Necessary?"** (Abe et al., **NeurIPS 2022**, arXiv:2202.06985): against a capacity-matched single model, a 4-member ResNet-18 ensemble vs a single **WideResNet-18-4** gives comparable **Brier 0.084±0.002 InD (CIFAR-10) / 0.210±0.002 OOD (CIFAR-10.1)** and near-identical accuracy (**~94.26% vs ~94.28% InD; ~86.10% vs ~86.26% OOD** — within ~0.02%/~0.16%); trend lines "correlations R>0.84, coefficients differ by 1% at most"; verbatim **"a single (but larger) neural network can replicate these qualities"** and **"ensemble diversity … does not meaningfully contribute … on OOD data."** Corroborating: **Model Soups** (Wortsman et al., ICML 2022, arXiv:2203.05482) recovers most ensemble gain at **1× inference cost** (ViT-G soup → **90.94% ImageNet top-1**, no extra cost). *[REMOVED from the first draft: the claim that Lobacheva 2007.08483 shows n=1 beating the ensemble — its actual finding is the opposite, so it is not a collapse case.]*

**(e) Pairwise-synergy / interaction metric.**
- **Shapley Interaction Index** (Grabisch & Roubens, *Int. J. Game Theory* 28(4):547–565, 1999): for {i,j}, average the discrete 2nd-order derivative δ_{ij}v(S)=v(S∪{i,j})−v(S∪{i})−v(S∪{j})+v(S) → **I_{ij} = Σ_{S⊆N\{i,j}} [|S|!(n−|S|−2)!/(n−1)!]·δ_{ij}v(S)**. Efficiency-respecting variant: **Shapley–Taylor** (Sundararajan et al., ICML 2020, arXiv:1902.05622). I_{ij}>0 synergy, <0 redundancy, ≈0 independent.
- **Ensemble diversity** (Kuncheva & Whitaker, *Machine Learning* 51(2):181–207, 2003) from 2×2 counts N¹¹,N¹⁰,N⁰¹,N⁰⁰: **Yule's Q** = (N¹¹N⁰⁰−N⁰¹N¹⁰)/(N¹¹N⁰⁰+N⁰¹N¹⁰) (Q=0 ⇔ independent); **Correlation ρ_{ij}** = (N¹¹N⁰⁰−N⁰¹N¹⁰)/√((N¹¹+N¹⁰)(N⁰¹+N⁰⁰)(N¹¹+N⁰¹)(N¹⁰+N⁰⁰)); **Disagreement** = (N⁰¹+N¹⁰)/total; **Double-fault** = N⁰⁰/total. Kuncheva & Whitaker's own caution: these correlate only weakly with realized ensemble accuracy — pair them with the ambiguity/covariance decomposition of (c), don't treat diversity as sufficient.

# Q5 — Independent Verification + Preregistration Recipe for a Matched-Compute Efficiency Claim

**(a) SESOI selection + published values.** Fix the SESOI *before* data collection and preregister it (**Lakens, Scheel & Isager 2018, AMPPS 1(2):259–269**), via: (1) **anchor-based** (smallest effect people notice); (2) **small-telescopes** (Simonsohn 2015, Psych Sci 26(5):559–569, DOI 10.1177/0956797614567341 — SESOI = effect giving the original study **33% power**); (3) **cost-benefit** (break-even where compute/$ saved exceeds adoption cost); (4) **MDE floor**. For a matched-compute claim, register the SESOI as a compute-normalized delta ("≥X% accuracy at equal FLOPs/wall-clock, or ≥Y% fewer FLOPs at equal accuracy") so a +0.3% blip is not promotable.
*Published:* **Anvari & Lakens 2021, JESP 96:104159** — anchor-based PANAS SESOI ≈ **0.26 scale points (PA) / 0.28 (NA)** for "a little" change (~small Cohen's d ≈0.3). *[Correction: the earlier "0.31 [0.26,0.36] / 0.25 [0.20,0.31]" is unsupported; use ~0.26/~0.28, CIs unverified.]* Small-telescopes = 33%-power effect. **ML-specific:** the literature typically does *not* register a numeric SESOI — arXiv:2511.19794 ("When +1% Is Not Enough," Nov 2025) argues "+1%" must be *tested*, not asserted; arXiv:2311.18807 recommends committing to metric/comparison/threshold before running *[unverified]*.

**(b) Honest small-n paired test at n=5.** Work on **paired per-seed deltas Δᵢ = variant − baseline under the same seed i** (same split, same compute).
- **Paired Student-t:** t = Δ̄/(s_Δ/√n), **df=4, t_crit(4, two-sided .05)=2.776** (one-sided 2.132) — valid only under ~normal independent deltas, fragile at n=5; treat as optimistic bound.
- **Sign test (n=5):** one-sided min p = (½)⁵ = **1/32 ≈ 0.031**; two-sided **2/32 = 0.0625** → clears one-sided .05 but **not** two-sided.
- **Wilcoxon signed-rank (n=5):** extreme configs each 1/32 → one-sided min p **1/32 ≈ 0.031**, two-sided **0.0625** (Demšar 2006, JMLR 7:1–30; Dietterich 1998 5×2cv).
- **Exact permutation (sign-flip) — recommended:** T_obs=Δ̄; enumerate all **2⁵=32** sign assignments; p = #{T≥T_obs}/32; **one-sided min p = 1/32 ≈ 0.031**, two-sided **0.0625** — the observed pattern always satisfies T≥T_obs so exact p is never 0. *[Correction: the Phipson–Smyth (2010) (b+1)/(m+1) correction (arXiv:1603.05766) applies to Monte-Carlo/randomly-drawn permutations — floor 1/33≈0.030 — not full enumeration's 1/32; don't mix conventions.]* Either way **two-sided .05 is impossible at n=5** (discrete floor 0.0625). Mirrors arXiv:2511.19794 at **k=3 seeds** (2³=8 → two-sided floor ≈0.25; "never declares significance" even when BCa intervals are strictly positive).
- **Bootstrap — legit vs pseudoreplicative:** legit = bootstrap **over independent seeds/runs** (BCa CIs on per-seed deltas, the companion to the permutation test); pseudoreplicative = bootstrap over **correlated clips/frames within one run** reporting a tiny CI as if n were thousands. Resample the experimental unit, not its correlated sub-units.

**(c) Pseudoreplication + claim ceiling.** **Hurlbert 1984, Ecological Monographs 54(2):187–211:** pseudoreplication = "inferential statistics to test for treatment effects … where either treatments are not replicated … or replicates are not statistically independent" (incidence **48% of studies applying inferential stats / 27% of 176 surveyed**). Governing concept = the **experimental unit** (smallest independently-treated unit); n = count of independent units. **With ~2 clips:** the clip is the experimental unit, frames are correlated sub-samples → **effective n = 2, not thousands of frames**; n=2 paired sign test gives one-sided min p = (½)² = 0.25 → essentially no inferential traction. Honest wording is bounded to **"consistent with" / "suggestive," never "demonstrates"/"significant";** frame/pixel bootstraps over 2 clips are the textbook clip-vs-frame error and must not manufacture a p-value.

**(d) Reproduction vs re-implementation — ACM badging.** **ACM Artifact Review & Badging v1.1 (Aug 2020):** **Results Reproduced** = main results "obtained in a subsequent study by a … team *other than the authors, using, in part, artifacts provided by the author*" → **independent REPRODUCTION (re-run author code).** **Results Replicated** = "*independently obtained … without the use of author-supplied artifacts*" → **independent RE-IMPLEMENTATION from specification.** **2020 swap:** in Aug 2020 (v1.1), after consulting NISO, ACM **interchanged "Reproduced" and "Replicated"** to match the rest of science (pre-2020 v1.0 had them reversed). Hierarchy: Repeatability (same team) → Reproducibility (different team, same artifacts → Reproduced) → Replicability (different team, independent re-impl → Replicated). NeurIPS/ML colloquial "reproducibility" (ML Reproducibility Challenge = re-implement) maps to ACM **Replicated** — name the badge explicitly. Tolerance is agreement-within-bounds, not bit-identical.

**(e) How many independent replications — is 3 defensible?** **Yes — 3 *independent* replications is a defensible promotion bar, but only if "independent" = 3 separate teams/pipelines each with adequate internal n, not 3 seeds in one pipeline.** ACM floor = **1** independent team (minimum, not sufficient for a contested claim). **Triangulation** (Munafò & Davey Smith 2018, Nature 553:399–401, DOI 10.1038/d41586-018-01023-3): robustness needs multiple lines with **independent/uncorrelated biases** — bias-independence, not raw count, is what matters; there is **no enshrined "exactly 3" rule**, it's pragmatic. Why 3 is the natural floor: (i) clears ACM's single-team bar with ≥2 corroborations; (ii) triangulates ≥3 bias-independent pipelines; (iii) matches the discrete-test arithmetic (sign-flip needs **n≥5 independent units** for one-sided p<.05; at n=4, 1/16=0.0625>.05), and k=3 seeds in one pipeline is deliberately under-powered.
**Promotion rule of thumb:** promote only when (1) a registered **SESOI is exceeded**, (2) each study clears an **honest small-n paired test** (exact permutation, one-sided, ≥5 independent seeds), and (3) **≥3 bias-independent reproductions/re-implementations** (ACM Reproduced and/or Replicated) triangulate the same direction and magnitude.
