## The loop

1. SCAN. Rebuild every category-1 and category-2 item plus the atlas local-to-10 tasks. Check moving
   files, live processes, source hashes, and receipts. Do not touch another live task's outputs.
2. PICK. Prefer the item that closes an open receipt chain, unblocks the most facets, supplies a
   missing control, or can kill the most expensive hypothesis.
3. PREFLIGHT. Require three healthy governor samples, exact task declaration, disk forecast, source
   and config identity, null, controls, units, stop rule, and restart path.
4. EXECUTE. Use one heavy lane and parallel light lanes. Resume exact work rather than restarting it.
5. VERIFY. Recompute through an independent path, mutate joins, attack controls, check finiteness,
   power, multiplicity, and claim scope.
6. RECORD. Write bound receipts and null cards. Update status and relevant facet cards.
7. REGENERATE. Refresh dependent ledgers in the order in the execution plan.
8. RECLASSIFY. Put the first remaining blocker in exactly one category. Do not infer hardware from a
   tier label or runtime estimate.
9. LOOP. Immediately select the next valid item. Queue human-only work without blocking other work.

## Studio gate

Do not create category 8 or 9 without all named evidence.

### Benefit gate

- surviving scientific effect;
- named independent-unit or context requirement;
- measured complete local profile;
- named decision deadline;
- parity-preserving next-rung pilot.

### Necessity gate

- three repeated valid local failures;
- all scientifically equivalent reductions attempted;
- a reduction changes the estimand or reverses the decision;
- smallest enabling memory, bandwidth, or latency rung calculated;
- larger-host pilot proves that the required experiment becomes possible.

Only non-factorizable resident state, scientifically necessary real-time latency, or synchronized
interaction that cannot be serialized without changing the estimand can earn necessity. Faster
seeds, clips, caches, or search earn at most throughput benefit.
